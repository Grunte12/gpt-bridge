# Scientific or medical visual

- State the audience, learning purpose, domain, scale, viewpoint, orientation,
  and whether the visual is schematic, anatomical, microscopic, exploded, or
  comparative.
- Provide the verified structures, labels, measurements, stages, and
  relationships. Require visible differentiation without distorting the
  underlying concept.
- Distinguish observation from simplification. Label conceptual, not-to-scale,
  or hypothetical content explicitly.
- Use an exact source image or trusted reference when morphology matters. Never
  infer diagnosis, dosage, safety limits, or novel findings from appearance.
- Verify labels and relationships independently. For high-stakes or
  publication-grade exactness, use the image as an illustration layer and add
  authoritative labels/data with deterministic tools.
- Forbid invented anatomy, impossible cross-sections, fake citations, fake
  measurements, decorative molecules, and watermarks.
